# SEO audit and search-visibility report (F051)

**Date:** 25 September 2026
**Scope:** Public SaveTheDates marketing site, before launch. Report only; no application or content changes.
**Role:** SEO & Growth, with engineering checks.

## Summary

- **The technical basics are sound.**
  - Only the homepage is indexable and listed in the sitemap.
  - Every private, example and error page is `noindex`.
  - Titles, descriptions and social tags are present.
  - Lighthouse scores 100 for SEO, accessibility and best practice.
  - Performance scores 100 on desktop and 92–93 on mobile.
  - No customer wedding page can be indexed.
- **The real obstacle is competition, not code.** Searches like "wedding website UK" and "online wedding RSVP" are held by large free platforms: Joy, Bridebook, Hitched and The Knot. One paid-product homepage is unlikely to outrank them soon.
- **The best opening is the save-the-date angle.** "Digital save the date" results are mostly card and file sellers (Etsy, Paperlust, Zazzle), not live links with Details and RSVP. The homepage never uses the phrases people search for: "digital save the date", "online RSVP", "WhatsApp", UK or Ireland.
- **The name "SaveTheDates" is a generic phrase.** People searching for it may get general "save the dates" results. Brand markup helps a little but cannot fully fix this.
- **Search volumes were not measured.** No keyword-volume tool was available. The keyword list below shows intent and who ranks today. Volume and difficulty need Google Keyword Planner, or Search Console data after launch.

## Findings

Severity is the effect on being found in search and choosing SaveTheDates. Effort is S (under an hour), M (a day or less) or L (more).

| # | Finding | Severity | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Head terms are held by free, well-established platforms | High (strategic) | — |
| 2 | Homepage copy misses the phrases couples search for | Medium | S–M |
| 3 | The brand name is a generic phrase | Medium | S (markup) |
| 4 | Section headings carry no descriptive meaning | Medium | S |
| 5 | The FAQ misses common search questions and objections | Medium | S–M |
| 6 | No Organization or WebSite structured data | Low | S |
| 7 | Mobile lab LCP is 3.2–3.3 s | Low | M |
| 8 | Theme preview images are larger than they are displayed | Low | S |
| 9 | Homepage is `private, no-store`, so it is not cached | Low | M |
| 10 | Small markup items | Low | S |

**1. Free platforms hold the main searches.** For "wedding website builder UK", "online wedding RSVP UK" and "wedding website Ireland", the results were:

- free platforms (Joy, Bridebook, Hitched, WeddingPlanner.co.uk, GettingMarried, The Knot, My RSVP);
- editorial "best free wedding website" lists (Bridebook, Wedissimo, The Wedding Edition);
- Irish directories (weddingsonline.ie, Confetti.ie).

Bridebook's page leads with "completely free" and Joy's H1 is "Your free wedding website—and so much more." A £29 product will not win "free wedding website" searches and should not target them. Treat head-term rankings as a long-term aim; expect early customers from other channels (F052).

**2. The homepage copy misses search phrases.** In the visible text (about 600 words), "wedding website" appears once (the H1), "save the date" 4 times and "RSVP" 5 times. "Digital save the date", "online RSVP", "WhatsApp", "UK", "Ireland" and "guest list" never appear. Yet sharing by WhatsApp and a single guest link are real features. The title, "Wedding websites, beautifully done | SaveTheDates", names the most competitive term and nothing that sets the product apart. Wording belongs in the homepage review (F053) so copy is changed once. One proposal for F053 to test: title "Digital save the date & wedding website with RSVP | SaveTheDates".

**3. The brand name is generic.** "Save the dates" is an everyday phrase, so a search for the brand may return general results. Google picks the site name automatically from the homepage and signals such as `WebSite` structured data on the domain root. Add `WebSite` markup (`name: "SaveTheDates"`, `url`), and keep the brand spelled the same everywhere. The domain choice (F041) matters more than markup here; a domain that matches the brand exactly helps most.

**4. Headings carry no meaning.** The H2s are "Find your kind of beautiful.", "A few details. A lovely hello.", "Everything for your day." and "Good to know." These suit the brand voice but tell search engines and skimmers nothing. Keep the tone, but let the heading or the line under it say what the section is: themes, how it works, pricing, FAQ.

**5. The FAQ misses common questions.** The four FAQs cover trying before paying, how guests RSVP, privacy and editing. Couples comparing options also ask:

- What happens after the wedding? (The six-month expiry is only in a footnote.)
- Can we get a refund?
- How is this different from a free wedding website?
- Can guests reply from their phone or WhatsApp?
- When should we send save the dates?

Answers must match the built product and the approved policies; the refund and expiry wording must wait for release-inputs. FAQ structured data no longer produces rich results for commercial sites (restricted to government and health sites since 14 September 2023), so add FAQs for readers, not for markup.

**6. No structured data.**

- Add `WebSite` to the homepage for the site name (finding 3).
- Add `Organization` with `name`, `url` and `logo` (`/icon-512.png`). Google says Organization markup does not give a rich result but helps it identify the business. Add contact details only once they are approved in release-inputs.
- Don't use `Product`/`Offer` markup yet: Google's product documentation is written for physical goods, and its eligibility for a service is unclear.

**7. Mobile lab LCP is slow.** Mobile LCP is 3.2–3.3 s across three runs. The LCP element is the hero `<h1>`, which uses system Georgia, not a web font. 86% of the time is render delay. Lighthouse flags the single 22 KB CSS bundle as render-blocking, with an estimated 230–320 ms saving. The desktop LCP is 0.7 s. These are simulated lab figures on a fast local server. Act only if real-user data in Search Console is "needs improvement", or when F032/F053 changes the hero anyway.

**8. Preview images are oversized.** The twelve theme previews use `sizes="(max-width: 600px) 190px, 250px"` but display at about 122–140 px wide on mobile. Lighthouse estimates 142–207 KiB could be saved. Tightening `sizes` in `phone-preview.tsx` is a small change.

**9. The homepage is not cached.** `/` is served dynamically with `Cache-Control: private, no-store` because it checks sign-in state to change two navigation labels. Locally the page takes 10 ms, so this costs little now. However, it rules out CDN caching and the browser back/forward cache (Lighthouse `bf-cache`), and every visit, including crawlers, reaches the Render instance. A static homepage with the signed-in label resolved in the browser is an option if traffic grows. Not a launch requirement.

**10. Small markup items.**

- `<html lang="en">` could be `en-GB` to match `og:locale`.
- The brand link's visible text "SaveTheDates FOR YOUR NEXT CHAPTER" differs from its `aria-label` "SaveTheDates home". Lighthouse flags this as `label-content-name-mismatch`, an accessibility issue.
- The 404 page sends two robots meta tags (`noindex` and `noindex, nofollow`). Google applies the stricter one, so this is harmless.
- When the privacy and terms pages exist (F041 update 3), link them from the footer. This builds trust, and Google OAuth brand verification needs it.

## Competitors (homepages, fetched 25 September 2026)

- **Bridebook** (`bridebook.com/uk/wedding-website`): H1 "Your wedding website, made easy". It is free and offers RSVP with dietary details, a gift list and accommodation. The page has feature sections with pictures, a six-question FAQ and app download links.
- **Joy** (`withjoy.com/wedding-website/?l=en-GB`): H1 "Your free wedding website—and so much more." Hundreds of templates, a detailed RSVP, a registry, a guest app and matching save the dates. Paid extras: print and custom domains.
- **Hitched** (`hitched.co.uk`): it appeared in the searches, but the page couldn't be fetched. Its free website builder is described in third-party lists.
- **Paperlust** (`paperlust.co/browse/save-the-date/online/`): H1 "Save the Date Online: Design Your Perfect First Announcement". A designed image or PDF you send yourself, at a flat price per order (shown as $35; currency not confirmed). No RSVP. The page is long: it compares digital and print, explains timing, etiquette and wording, and has an FAQ.

What this means:

- Competitors win on free, breadth and content depth. SaveTheDates can't match free or breadth, and shouldn't claim to.
- Its honest difference is narrow: a save the date that is a live link, sent in one message and opening straight to Details and RSVP, for one payment.
- Paperlust shows how helpful timing and wording content can win the save-the-date searches.

## Content and structure

- **Is one indexed homepage enough?** For launch, yes. For growth, no: a single page can't match several different search intents. Add pages only when each has content of its own (below).
- **Should the theme examples be indexed?** No. The twelve examples share the same fictional text and differ only in CSS, so indexing them would create near-duplicate thin pages about fictional couples. Keep them `noindex`. If Search Console later shows searches for themes, build one indexable themes gallery page instead.
- **Focused pages or guides worth considering** (owner decision; all hand-written and grounded in the product):
  1. **Digital save the date page.** What a link-based save the date is, how it is sent by WhatsApp or text, and what guests see: Details and RSVP. This is the strongest candidate.
  2. **"When to send save the dates" guide (UK/Ireland).** Timing, what to include, and sending by message. Linked from the homepage FAQ.
  3. **An online RSVP page:** only if Search Console shows RSVP impressions. Otherwise it would repeat the homepage.
  - Don't make pages per town or venue, "free" pages, or comparison pages naming competitors.

## Links from other sites (suggestions only)

- **Editorial "best wedding website" lists in the UK and Ireland:** Wedissimo, The Wedding Edition, Country House Weddings, Confetti.ie, the weddingsonline.ie blog. Pitch them honestly once the site is live, with the one-payment, save-the-date-first angle.
- **Northern Ireland and Irish wedding suppliers and venues:** partnerships where a link helps their couples. This overlaps F052.
- **A "Made with SaveTheDates" credit on guest pages** is an owner decision in F052. Guest pages are `noindex`, so the value is guests seeing it, not search ranking.
- Don't buy links or swap links. Google's spam policies penalise link schemes.

## After launch: monthly Search Console check

Setup is F041 step 7. Also add the site to Bing Webmaster Tools, which can import from Search Console. It is free, and Bing results also appear in other search products.

Each month:

1. **Pages report.** Only marketing URLs are indexed. Any `/examples/…`, account or wedding URL showing as indexed is an incident: check its `noindex` right away.
2. **Performance.** Record impressions, clicks, average position and click-through rate for the searches in the keyword table, and note new searches worth a page.
3. **Core Web Vitals.** Check once data appears. Act on finding 7 if mobile LCP is not "good".
4. **Sitemaps, manual actions and security issues** show no errors.
5. Record a short line in the F009 or operations log. There's no need for a new document.

## Follow-up, 25 September 2026 (after the owner's decisions)

- **Domain.** `savethedates.co.uk`, recorded in `release-inputs.md`. The canonical origin is `https://savethedates.co.uk`, and `www` redirects to it. It matches the brand exactly, which helps finding 3 most.

Premium designs and print are paid extras.
  - So "free" is taken.
  - The difference we can honestly claim is the shape of the product: the save the date *is* the link, sent in one message, opening straight to Details and RSVP, for one payment.
  - The new page states only our own features and names no competitors.
- **Done.**
  - F058: structured data, `en-GB`, brand-link label, image `sizes`, a smaller homepage stylesheet, off-screen theme cards skipped, and monitoring loaded after first paint.
  - F059: homepage wording (findings 2, 4 and 5) and the digital save the date page, the report's first content recommendation.
  - The "when to send save the dates" guide is still trigger-based. For now the question is answered in both FAQs.
