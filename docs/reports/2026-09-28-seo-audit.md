# SaveTheDates SEO audit — 28 September 2026

**Audited:** https://savethedates.co.uk, its alternate/staging hosts, and source at `4410c9df9f6142c1f212d97f0a5aadb437f2700f`.
**Scope:** Report only. Technical SEO, content and search intent, mobile rendering, performance, structured data, internal links, search measurement, and acquisition opportunities. No application changes, deployment, account submissions, outreach or customer-data access.
**Status:** Live-site audit complete within the evidence limits below. Search Console, real-user performance and keyword/backlink metrics remain unverified. This supersedes the current-state conclusions of the [25 September audit](2026-09-25-seo-audit.md), which remains a historical record.

## Assessment

The app has a sound technical SEO foundation. The three acquisition pages are publicly reachable, server-rendered, indexable and self-canonicalized. The sitemap includes all three. Structured data and social metadata exist, the main domain redirects work, and sampled account/example/guest-error routes are excluded from indexing. No critical indexing blocker was found on the public acquisition pages.

There are worthwhile improvements. The strongest measured issue is mobile performance on **`/what-we-offer`**, where three Lighthouse runs consistently produced **82/100 performance and approximately 4.93 seconds LCP**. Preview images also remained blank in some browser checks and need investigation. Beyond that, consolidate the public Render hostname, distinguish the three pages' search purposes more clearly, and establish actual indexing/query/conversion measurements before investing in many more landing pages.

**A Lighthouse SEO score of 100 is a check of selected technical basics, not a ranking forecast or proof of Google indexing.** This site launched on 26 September; a two-day-old public site cannot yet be judged by established organic traffic trends.

### SEO-01: reduce showcase loading work

The first showcase run transferred approximately **895 KiB**, versus **328 KiB** for the homepage and **288 KiB** for the digital page. Its trace fetched all four embedded example documents, a roughly **307 KiB** lake photo and a roughly **145 KiB** decorative olive image. The LCP element was the **hero H1**, with render delay dominating the simulated breakdown. It is not established that a single large image causes the slow H1 paint.

`src/features/marketing/offer-showcase.tsx` mounts real guest pages in iframes. Native lazy loading is already present on some frames, but did not prevent all four from loading during these tests. Lighthouse estimates 234 KiB of image savings and flags repeated frame JavaScript. Its 594 KiB unused-JavaScript estimate includes repeated frame entries and must not be treated as guaranteed unique network savings.

Recommended engineering sequence:

1. Profile the initial frame loading and H1 render delay under the same mobile conditions.
2. Use lightweight static previews for decorative phones, or mount those frames only near the viewport. Keep the interactive RSVP demonstration available when requested or reached.
3. Serve appropriately compressed/sized **fictional public demo** photos and graphics. Keep private customer photos' existing storage/authorization boundaries intact.
4. Avoid booting unnecessary app/monitoring code repeatedly inside decorative frames where feasible.
5. Repeat three mobile runs; check LCP, payload and demo usability. Target LCP ≤2.5 s and retain server-rendered feature copy.

Source pointers: `offer-showcase.tsx` (`OfferPhone`), `src/features/weddings/wedding-photo.tsx`, `src/app/what-we-offer/phone/[theme]/`, and the route's CSS imports. No CDN or framework migration is needed to begin this work.

### SEO-03: reproduce and resolve blank previews

The digital page's two hero previews stayed blank at 390 and 1440 px in bundled Chromium after a 20-second wait. Installed Chrome reproduced this at 390 px with a 15-second wait; its homepage hero loaded correctly. The images remained incomplete, rather than returning a recorded HTTP error. Mobile Lighthouse at its different pixel ratio successfully loaded the 384-pixel variants. Direct requests for both 256-pixel variants returned 200 in approximately 0.16 s.

This is a reproducible browser observation in this audit, **not** a proven permanent origin outage. Inspect network/preload/srcset behavior, browser conditions and optimization variants before choosing a fix. Validate in a normal mobile browser and a clean desktop browser. Ensure decorative previews either load reliably or have a useful fallback. These previews communicate the product's value, so blank phones matter even when text remains crawlable.

Evidence: `test-results/seo-audit-2026-09-28/{image-check,chrome-check}.json`, corresponding `*-hero-loaded.png`/`chrome-digital-390.png`, and desktop Lighthouse unfinished requests.

Acquisition pages send `Cache-Control: private, no-store, max-age=0` and are dynamically rendered for signed-in navigation. Sampled anonymous document requests were fast; caching was not the dominant measured problem. Consider cacheable anonymous marketing rendering only after the showcase/image work. Never cache personalized account navigation or wedding data publicly. Source: `src/app/page.tsx`, marketing `session.ts`, and `src/proxy.ts`.

## Canonical-host recommendation

The Render hostname's homepage and digital page both return 200, `index, follow`, and main-domain canonicals. There is no evidence that Google indexed this alternate host, and this is not a demonstrated duplicate-content penalty.

Prefer a permanent redirect for public page GET/HEAD requests from that known hostname to the matching `https://savethedates.co.uk` path, preserving necessary query parameters. Review health checks, webhook endpoints and authentication callbacks before applying a blanket host rule. Validate one-hop redirects, no loop, and unchanged primary-domain behavior. Do not replace the correct canonical with noindex on the main domain. [Google canonicalization guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls).

## Structured data, images and international SEO

- **Keep the existing WebSite and Organization markup.** It uses the correct domain and a reachable logo. Optional refinements are stable `@id` values, the published support email, and `sameAs` links to confirmed official profiles. Do not invent a business address, reviews or social accounts. Organization data helps identify the entity; it does not guarantee a rich result. [Google Organization guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization).
- **Do not prioritize FAQ rich-result markup.** Google's documented FAQ rich-result eligibility is restricted to authoritative government/health sites. Continue writing FAQs for couples. [Google FAQ policy](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes).
- **Do not promise rating stars from app markup.** Google's SoftwareApplication rich-result requirements include a qualifying rating or review as well as price and name. None was established in this audit. Any later markup must accurately describe the paid offer and satisfy the current requirements; never manufacture ratings. [Google software-app requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app).
- **Image semantics are appropriate in sampled markup.** No sampled HTML images lacked an `alt` attribute. Decorative phone previews deliberately have empty alt text and are hidden from assistive technology, with design names supplied beside them; adding keywords to these images would not help users. Keep descriptive alt text for meaningful standalone product screenshots.
- **English/UK signals are coherent.** `lang="en-GB"`, `og:locale=en_GB`, GBP pricing and the `.co.uk` domain fit current positioning. There are no alternate language versions requiring hreflang. Avoid adding hreflang or country pages without real localized content.
- **Legal sitemap entries are optional.** Add them if the intended policy is to list all indexable first-party pages; their current omission is not a blocker. A sitemap does not need fabricated `lastmod` dates or special priority tuning. [Google sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap).

### First 30–90 days

Review search performance weekly during initial discovery, then monthly. Record clicks, impressions, CTR and position by **page + query**, split brand/non-brand and UK/Ireland where sample sizes allow. Look for the wrong page appearing for a query before concluding that pages compete with one another. Check indexing exclusions and actual Core Web Vitals when data becomes available.

The reviewed analytics path allowlist excludes customer guest links and strips query strings. Preserve that privacy boundary. Public/account-entry page views alone do not establish completed signups or paid purchases attributable to SEO. Determine whether existing reporting can provide aggregate organic landing visits, successful signups and purchases; if not, design minimal conversion measurement separately. Never send names, guest secrets, replies, dietary information or payment details into analytics.

Choose one content improvement from the query map, observe its impressions and qualified traffic, and then expand. Establish a real baseline before setting traffic or revenue targets; this audit cannot responsibly forecast a percentage ranking uplift.

### Later, if evidence justifies it

Consider one original guide, one stronger designs gallery, expanded verified business markup or cacheable anonymous marketing rendering. Reassess actual demand and performance before creating more infrastructure or pages. Preserve noindex for customer and fictional guest routes throughout.
